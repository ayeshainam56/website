# What is the Difference Between Form I-864, I-864A and I-864EZ?

When a U.S. sponsor files a family-based immigrant petition, one of the most important (and legally binding) documents is the **Affidavit of Support**. USCIS uses this form to make sure the sponsored immigrant won’t become a public charge. There are three closely related forms you may see: **Form I-864 (Affidavit of Support)**, **Form I-864A (Contract Between Sponsor and Household Member)**, and **Form I-864EZ (shorter affidavit option)**. Knowing which form to use — and why — prevents RFEs and costly delays. 
---

## Key Takeaways
- **I-864** is the main Affidavit of Support the sponsor files in most family-based and some employment-based immigrant cases.   
- **I-864A** is a contract a household member signs when their income/assets are counted toward the sponsor’s financial requirement.  
- **I-864EZ** is a shorter, simplified affidavit that can only be used in limited situations (single beneficiary, sponsor is petitioner, income from W-2s/pension only). 
- Use the **I-864P HHS Poverty Guidelines** current to the filing year to calculate the minimum income needed. 

---

## 1. What is Form I-864 (Affidavit of Support)?

**Form I-864** is the standard Affidavit of Support under INA §213A. When you sign I-864 you accept a legal obligation to financially support the immigrant so they do not become dependent on public benefits. Sponsors must normally demonstrate income/assets at **125% of the HHS poverty guidelines** (100% in some limited cases). The obligation generally continues until the immigrant becomes a U.S. citizen, has 40 qualifying quarters of work, dies, or permanently leaves the U.S. 

**Who files I-864?**
- The petitioner (U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident) who filed the immigrant visa or adjustment petition, or a joint sponsor if the petitioner’s income is insufficient. 

**Why it matters:** I-864 is a legally enforceable contract — failure to meet the obligations can result in enforcement actions by the sponsored immigrant or, in some cases, by government agencies. 

---

## 2. What is Form I-864A (Contract Between Sponsor and Household Member)?

**Form I-864A** is used when a household member (for example, a spouse, adult child, or roommate who lives in the sponsor’s household) agrees to make their income and/or assets available to help the sponsor meet the financial requirement. By signing I-864A the household member becomes jointly liable with the sponsor under INA §213A. 

**Common uses of I-864A:**
- Sponsor’s own income is not enough; a spouse or parent living in the same household signs I-864A to combine incomes.  
- Household member’s W-2 wages or assets are necessary to reach the required poverty guideline threshold.

**Important:** I-864A is **not** a substitute for I-864. The sponsor still files I-864; the household member only completes I-864A to promise availability of income/assets. 

---

## 3. What is Form I-864EZ (Shorter Affidavit of Support)?

**Form I-864EZ** is a streamlined version of I-864 for simple, straightforward cases. You may use I-864EZ **only if all** the following apply:  
- The sponsor is the petitioner (the person who filed the immigrant petition),  
- There is **only one** intending immigrant (one beneficiary) on the petition, and  
- The sponsor’s income is based entirely on salary or pension shown on one or more Forms W-2 (no complex income sources, no need for a joint sponsor, no household member income used). 

**Why choose I-864EZ?** It’s shorter and faster to complete, but it’s available only when strict conditions are met — otherwise USCIS directs you to complete the full Form I-864. 
---

## 4. When to Use Each Form — Simple Flow

- Sponsor has adequate income by themselves, is the petitioner, and there is one beneficiary **and** income is W-2/pension only → **I-864EZ**. 
- Sponsor is the petitioner but uses a household member’s income → **I-864 (sponsor)** **+ I-864A (household member)**.  
- Sponsor needs a joint sponsor because they don’t meet the income requirement → **I-864 (sponsor)** + **I-864 (joint sponsor)** (no I-864A unless household member’s income is used). 
---

## 5. Common Mistakes That Cause Delays

- **Submitting I-864EZ when you don’t meet every requirement.** USCIS will reject or issue an RFE if a condition is not satisfied — use the full I-864 when in doubt. 
- **Forgetting I-864A when you counted household income.** If you rely on a household member’s income, they **must** sign I-864A; otherwise your packet is incomplete.  
- **Using outdated poverty guidelines.** Always use the current **I-864P** HHS poverty figures for the year you file. 

---

## Checklist — What to Include with an Affidavit of Support

- Completed and signed **Form I-864** (and I-864A or I-864EZ if applicable).  
- Sponsor’s federal tax returns (past 3 years) and W-2s or pay stubs.  
- Proof of U.S. domicile for the sponsor.  
- Evidence of household member income if using I-864A (pay stubs, tax returns).  
- If using assets to supplement income: evidence of ownership and valuation documents.  
- Correct HHS poverty guideline table (Form I-864P) used in calculations. 

---

## FAQs

**Q: Can a joint sponsor sign I-864A?**  
No — a joint sponsor signs their own **I-864**. I-864A is only for household members who are not the primary sponsor. 

**Q: Does divorce end the sponsor’s obligations under I-864?**  
No. Divorce does **not** end the sponsor’s legal obligation in most cases (the obligation lasts until the immigrant becomes a U.S. citizen, accumulates 40 quarters, dies, or permanently leaves the U.S.). 

**Q: What if the sponsor doesn’t meet income but has a household member in the U.S. willing to help?**  
Have that household member complete **Form I-864A** so their income may be used to meet the requirement — and remember their signature makes them legally liable too.

---

## Final Thoughts

Choosing the correct affidavit form is a small but critical step that affects case completeness and timing. When the facts are simple, **I-864EZ** can save time; when multiple incomes, household members, or joint sponsors are involved, **I-864** (with **I-864A** where applicable) is usually required. Double-check the USCIS instructions, use the current poverty guidelines, and include all supporting evidence to avoid RFEs.
