# VAWA Prima Facie Approval What It Actually Means (And What It Doesn’t)

Getting a **Notice of Prima Facie Case (NPFC)** after filing a VAWA self-petition (Form I-360) is a relief and also confusing. Many survivors read *“prima facie”* and think the case is approved. It isn’t. Prima facie is an **early, preliminary finding** that your packet *appears* to meet the basic legal requirements, not a final decision. 

Below is a practical, survivor-focused explanation of what prima facie does, what it doesn’t do, and the exact next steps you should take after you receive the notice.

---

## Key takeaways (quick)
- **Prima facie = preliminary.** It means USCIS’s first review found enough evidence **on its face** to suggest your self-petition meets VAWA elements but USCIS still conducts a full adjudication.  
- **It can unlock services.** A prima facie notice often helps survivors access certain public benefits and community services that require proof of a qualifying immigration filing. Local agencies commonly accept NPFC as evidence.  
- **It is not work authorization or permanent status.** NPFC does **not** itself grant a green card or an Employment Authorization Document (EAD). Work authorization usually requires filing I-765 under an eligible category (or later steps after I-360 approval/adjustment). Check requirements carefully.   
- **Keep the NPFC safe and use it promptly.** You may need it to access benefits, housing, or social services while your I-360 is fully adjudicated.

---

## 1 What “prima facie” actually means (plain language)
USCIS policy describes a **prima facie review** as an initial check to determine whether the evidence in your self-petition appears sufficient on its face to meet the basic legal elements (relationship, battery/extreme cruelty, residence, and good moral character where required). If that test is met, USCIS issues an NPFC. This is a helpful administrative finding but it’s not the final legal decision on your I-360. 

---

## 2 What benefits & practical uses the NPFC can provide
- **Access to certain public benefits or services.** Many state and local benefit programs, victim-service organizations, and resettlement agencies accept an NPFC as proof you’ve filed a qualifying self-petition and may provide immediate help (housing, cash assistance, counseling) while USCIS completes its review. Policies vary by program and state always check locally. 
- **Evidence for safety planning and shelter intake.** Domestic-violence shelters and nonprofits frequently request the NPFC to document eligibility for specialized services.  
- **Support for other applications.** An NPFC can support fee-waiver requests or help establish eligibility for community-based assistance programs tied to immigration filings.

> Important: acceptance of NPFC for public benefits depends on the **agency** some programs accept the NPFC; others require additional documents. Always verify with the benefits agency.

---

## 3 What NPFC **doesn’t** do
- **Not a final approval.** USCIS can still issue an RFE, deny the I-360, or request more evidence after prima facie. The NPFC simply shows the initial packet passes a surface check.   
- **Not automatic work authorization.** The NPFC alone is not an EAD. To work lawfully you must qualify under an EAD eligibility category and file Form I-765 (or obtain work authorization through later approvals like an adjusted status). Read I-765 guidance carefully for which supporting evidence is needed.  
- **Not permanent evidence of status.** The NPFC may have limited validity for third-party programs it does not change your immigration status or create lawful permanent residency.

---

## 4 Timing & renewals what to expect
- **When you’ll typically get it:** In many cases applicants receive prima facie notice **within several months** of filing, but timing varies widely by service center and caseload. Real-world reports show the NPFC often arrives before biometrics or while your case is in initial review.  
- **Validity & renewals:** Some practitioners report NPFC letters include a validity period or are renewed administratively while the I-360 is pending; practices can differ and local agencies may accept extensions differently. If your NPFC lapses and your I-360 is still pending, ask USCIS or your service provider for guidance. 

---

## 5 Practical next steps after you receive a prima facie notice
1. **Save the NPFC immediately.** Scan multiple copies and save in a secure folder (phone + cloud + attorney file).  
2. **Use it for urgent needs.** Show the NPFC to shelters, victim-service organizations, or local benefit offices that accept prima facie evidence. Ask the agency what additional proof they require.  
3. **Don’t assume the case is finished.** Continue to prepare for possible RFEs or interview requests: collect corroborating evidence (medical records, police reports, affidavits, photos).  
4. **If you applied for an EAD, check the I-765 rules.** Filing I-765 without meeting the eligibility basis or required evidence can cause denial talk to counsel if you need to apply for work authorization while waiting.  
5. **Tell your attorney and service providers.** If you have counsel, inform them immediately so they can track the case, respond to any RFEs, and use the NPFC where helpful.  
6. **If you need benefits and an agency refuses the NPFC, get written denial.** That helps appeals or alternative requests later.

## 6 Common questions survivors ask (FAQs)

**Q Does prima facie guarantee I’ll get the green card?**  
**A:** No. Prima facie is a helpful early finding but USCIS still completes full adjudication; final approval depends on all evidence and possible later reviews.

**Q Can I work if I only have an NPFC?**  
**A:** No. NPFC alone is not EAD. To work lawfully you must file or qualify under an appropriate Form I-765 category (or later receive a work permit after adjustment/approval). Always check I-765 instructions for the exact evidence required. 

**Q How long does NPFC last?**  
**A:** There’s no single national rule on renewals for third-party acceptance USCIS issues NPFCs during pendency and some providers report administrative renewals. If a benefits program asks for a current NPFC and yours is older, contact your attorney or the agency for direction. 

**Q I got an NPFC and then an RFE what now?**  
**A:** RFEs are common. Respond fully, on time, and with the exact evidence requested. If you need help, seek legal assistance an organized RFE response greatly improves your chance of success.

- [ ] Continue gathering supporting evidence (medical, police, affidavits).  
- [ ] Track USCIS case status and respond quickly to RFEs or biometrics notices.  
- [ ] If you plan to seek work authorization, review I-765 rules and file under the correct category with supporting evidence.
