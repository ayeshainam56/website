# Top 5 USCIS Forms Every Immigrant Should Know in 2025

There are hundreds of USCIS forms — but five forms come up again and again in real client cases. Whether you’re sponsoring family, waiting on a green card, trying to work while you wait, planning to travel, or replacing a lost card, understanding these forms will save time, reduce stress, and prevent avoidable rejections.

This guide explains **what each form does**, **who files it**, **common mistakes to avoid**, and practical checklists so your next filing is complete and correct.

---

## Quick snapshot — the five forms
1. **Form I-130** — Petition for Alien Relative (family sponsorship).  
2. **Form I-485** — Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status (green card inside the U.S.).  
3. **Form I-765** — Application for Employment Authorization (work permit / EAD).  
4. **Form I-131** — Application for Travel Documents (Advance Parole, Reentry Permit, Refugee Travel Document).  
5. **Form I-90** — Application to Replace Permanent Resident Card (renew or replace Green Card).

---

## Why these matter
These forms map to the common life-cycle of immigration cases: sponsor a relative (I-130), become a permanent resident (I-485), work while waiting (I-765), travel safely during processing (I-131), and keep your physical green card valid (I-90). Mistakes on these forms are frequent but avoidable — knowing the rules reduces delays and the risk of RFEs or rejections.

---

## Form I-130 — Petition for Alien Relative

**What it does:** Starts a family-based immigration case by proving a qualifying relationship exists between a U.S. sponsor and a foreign relative.

**Who files:** The U.S. sponsor (citizen or lawful permanent resident).

**When to use it:** To sponsor a spouse, child, parent, or sibling (eligibility depends on petitioner status).

**Key tips**
- Submit strong proof of relationship (marriage certificate, birth certificates, photos, joint financial documents).  
- Include certified translations for any non-English documents.  
- Check the correct filing address — USCIS filing locations change.  

**Common mistakes**
- Missing or unsigned signatures.  
- Using an outdated form edition.  
- Weak or missing evidence of the family relationship.

---

## Form I-485 — Adjustment of Status

**What it does:** Allows an eligible person already in the U.S. to apply for lawful permanent residence (green card) without leaving the country.

**Who files:** The foreign national seeking to adjust status.

**When to use it:** When an immigrant visa is immediately available or the applicant is in an eligible category (e.g., immediate relative of a U.S. citizen).

**Key tips**
- Verify that your **priority date is current** (when required) before filing.  
- File supporting forms (I-765 for work, I-131 for travel) if you need work or travel authorization while the I-485 is pending.  
- Follow current USCIS rules for the medical exam (Form I-693) — timing and submission rules change.  

**Common mistakes**
- Filing when priority date is not current.  
- Traveling without Advance Parole (if required) — this can abandon your I-485.  
- Inconsistent information across petition and adjustment forms.

---

## Form I-765 — Employment Authorization Document (EAD)

**What it does:** Requests permission to work in the U.S. and obtains the physical EAD card (Form I-766).

**Who files:** Asylum applicants, adjustment applicants, parolees, TPS recipients, and other eligible categories.

**Key tips**
- **Choose the correct eligibility category (Item 27).** The wrong code causes denials.  
- Renew early — file timely to avoid employment gaps. Certain categories may qualify for automatic extension if a timely renewal is filed.  
- Include two passport-style photos and required supporting evidence.

**Common mistakes**
- Wrong eligibility code.  
- Late renewal filings leading to work interruptions.  
- Missing photos or incorrect payment amount.

---

## Form I-131 — Travel Documents (Advance Parole & Reentry Permits)

**What it does:** Requests Advance Parole (travel while I-485 is pending), a reentry permit (for green card holders traveling long-term), or a refugee travel document.

**Who files:** Adjustment applicants (Advance Parole), lawful permanent residents (reentry permit), refugees/asylees (travel document).

**Key tips**
- **Do not travel without Advance Parole** if your I-485 requires it — doing so can be treated as abandonment.  
- File early if you plan international travel; emergency travel may be expedited in limited circumstances.  
- Follow filing instructions carefully for biometrics and fees.

**Common mistakes**
- Traveling before Advance Parole is approved.  
- Choosing the wrong travel-document option.  
- Missing biometrics appointments.

---

## Form I-90 — Replace or Renew Your Green Card

**What it does:** Renew an expiring green card or replace a lost, stolen, or damaged Permanent Resident Card.

**Who files:** Lawful permanent residents.

**Key tips**
- File up to **180 days before expiration** for renewals to avoid gaps in ID/employment eligibility.  
- Use USCIS online filing for faster processing and tracking.  
- If your name changed, include legal documents showing the change.

**Common mistakes**
- Waiting until after the card expires to file.  
- Using the wrong reason code on the form.  
- Failing to attend required biometrics.

## Filing checklist (copy/paste for clients)
- Use the **current USCIS edition** of any form — USCIS can reject older versions.  
- Include government-issued ID copies and certified translations for non-English documents.  
- Include the correct fee (use the USCIS Fee Calculator) and the correct filing address.  
- Sign every required page and keep digital + paper copies of the full packet.  
- Track receipt numbers and set calendar reminders for biometrics, interviews, and renewals.

---

## Short FAQs

**Q: Can I file I-765 and I-485 together?**  
A: Often yes — many applicants file I-765 (EAD) concurrently with I-485. Check eligibility and include required supporting evidence.

**Q: How long do these forms take?**  
A: Processing times vary by form, filing location, and workload. Use USCIS processing times and your receipt number to track status.

**Q: Where do I file each form?**  
A: Filing addresses vary by form and category. Always check the USCIS website for the current mailing address or e-filing options before you submit.

---

## Final tips to avoid delays
1. **Don’t guess the fee** — use the USCIS fee calculator and include exact payment.  
2. **File current editions only** — USCIS will reject old PDFs.  
3. **Prepare strong supporting evidence** (relationships, finances, medical records).  
4. **Keep copies and confirmations** — receipts start critical clocks.  
5. **When in doubt, consult an attorney** — a brief review can prevent months of delay.
