# Types of Status Change: Student Visa, Visa Extension, Naturalization (2025 Guide)

Changing or extending immigration status can feel confusing — different forms, different timelines, and serious consequences if you get it wrong. This practical guide explains the main types of status changes people ask about: changing nonimmigrant status or extending a stay (Form **I-539**), student-specific pathways (F-1, OPT/STEM), and becoming a U.S. citizen through **naturalization (Form N-400)**. I’ll highlight the critical filing rules, common traps, and best next steps so you (or your client) won’t be surprised.

---

## Key takeaways (quick)

- Use **Form I-539** to request an extension of stay or change of nonimmigrant status in many—but not all—cases. Read the instructions carefully: some employment-based changes use Form I-129 instead.  
- USCIS generally expects requests for extension or change **before your current status expires**; failing to maintain status can make approval unlikely. File early and include strong supporting evidence.   
- F-1 students have special rules: post-completion **OPT** and the **STEM OPT 24-month extension** exist but have strict eligibility and timing requirements. Always coordinate with your DSO. 
- Naturalization (N-400) typically requires **5 years** of permanent residence (3 years in some spousal cases) and you may file **90 days** before meeting the residence requirement—check USCIS guidance and continuous residence rules. 
- Policy is evolving (D/S proposals, new vetting procedures). Because rules and filing windows can change, consult an immigration attorney for time-sensitive or complex matters. 
---

## 1. Change of Nonimmigrant Status vs. Extension of Stay — what’s the difference?

- **Extension of stay** = asking USCIS to let you remain in the *same* nonimmigrant classification longer than originally authorized (for example, extending a B-1/B-2 stay).  
- **Change of status (COS)** = asking USCIS to change your classification while you remain in the U.S. (for example, from B-2 visitor to F-1 student).  

Both requests are commonly filed on **Form I-539**, *Application to Extend/Change Nonimmigrant Status*, but **not all categories use I-539** (certain employment classifications require Form I-129 instead). Read the I-539 filing instructions and USCIS tips before filing. 

**Practical rule:** Always file before your current authorized stay expires. USCIS is unlikely to approve an extension or change if you “fell out of status” before filing, except in narrow discretionary situations where you can document why the delay was unavoidable. 

---

## 2. Timing & consequences — file early and keep proof

- **When to file:** File the extension/change *before* the I-94 expiration or program end date (for students) shown on your admission record. USCIS has specific filing addresses and rules for I-539; follow them exactly. 
- **If you overstay:** Remaining in the U.S. past your authorized stay can trigger bars to future admission, removal exposure, and denial of your requests. Extensions filed after status expires are evaluated harshly. 

**Tip:** Keep proof of timely filing (USPS tracking, FedEx receipt, or USCIS e-file confirmation) and maintain copies of all documents you submit.

---

## 3. Student issues — F-1, OPT, STEM OPT, and proposed changes to D/S

### F-1 basics
- F-1 students are admitted for **Duration of Status (D/S)**, generally tied to the program end date and SEVIS records. You must maintain full-time enrollment and timely address/reporting compliance. DSOs (Designated School Officials) are your on-campus point of contact. 

### OPT & STEM OPT
- **Post-completion OPT** allows eligible F-1 grads temporary work related to their degree. Eligible STEM graduates can apply for an additional **24-month STEM OPT extension** if they meet employer and training plan requirements. Timing, employer E-Verify requirements, and filing windows are strict. 

### Policy notes
- Proposed DHS rulemaking in 2025 seeks to replace flexible “Duration of Status” with fixed admission periods for F/J nonimmigrants — if finalized, this could mean more frequent filings to extend or change status for students. Watch rulemaking and consult school DSOs about SEVIS/compliance changes.

**Practical student tips:** Coordinate any COS or extension with your DSO, file timely, and preserve copies of I-20s, SEVIS records, and employment authorizations.

## 4. Naturalization (Form N-400) — the path to U.S. citizenship

- **Who can file:** Most applicants must be a lawful permanent resident (green card holder) for **5 years** (or **3 years** if married to a U.S. citizen and meeting other conditions). You may file **90 days before** completing the required continuous residence period in many cases. Use USCIS’s naturalization eligibility tools and Form N-400 instructions to confirm timing. 

- **Other requirements:** Good moral character, English and civics testing (with some exceptions), continuous residence and physical presence requirements, and selective service compliance (if applicable). USCIS recently announced tighter vetting and some procedural changes—stay current on agency guidance. 

**Pro tip:** Gather tax returns, travel records, and proof of residence before filing. Small travel mistakes or missing documentation commonly delay N-400 adjudication.

---

## 5. Common mistakes that delay or derail requests

1. **Filing late** — missing the I-94/program end date before filing.  
2. **Using the wrong form** — e.g., trying to change to an employer-based classification that requires Form I-129.   
3. **Incomplete evidence** — missing pay stubs, DSOs letters, or proof of relationship.  
4. **Counting on policy stability** — proposed regulatory changes (e.g., D/S replacement) may alter timing; plan conservatively.   
5. **Assuming naturalization is automatic after green card** — meet continuous residence, physical presence, and other N-400 requirements before filing. 

---

## 6. Quick filing checklist

**For I-539 (extension/change):**
- Completed Form I-539 (or electronic filing confirmation).   
- Copy of current I-94 and passport photo page.  
- Proof of status maintenance (I-20 for students, approval notices, employer letters if applicable).  
- Filing fee and Form I-539A (if co-applicants). 

**For student/OPT/STEM:**
- Current I-20, DSO recommendation, OPT application evidence, employer E-Verify info (for STEM). 

**For N-400 naturalization:**
- Completed N-400, fee, biometric notice, tax returns, travel history, and proof of residence. Check the N-400 instructions for exceptions and fee waivers.
