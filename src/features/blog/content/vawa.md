# VAWA Protection When Your Abuser Has a Green Card

I want to clear up a misunderstanding I see every week in my office. Many immigrants believe VAWA only helps if your abuser is a U.S. citizen. That's wrong. 

**The truth:** VAWA absolutely works if your abuser is a green card holder. Here's what you should know.

## Key Differences You'll Face

1. **Timing for Your Green Card**
   - *If abuser is citizen*: Can adjust status immediately after VAWA approval
   - *If abuser is green card holder*: Must wait for visa availability (often 2-3 years)

2. **Evidence Needs**
   - You'll need proof of their status (copy of green card or I-485 approval)
   - Stronger relationship evidence helps (shared bills, photos, witness statements)

## Required Documents Checklist

For your I-360 petition:

- [ ] Copy of abuser's green card (front/back)
- [ ] Your marriage certificate
- [ ] Proof of cohabitation (lease/utility bills)
- [ ] Evidence of abuse (any of these):
- [ ] Police reports
- [ ] Medical records
- [ ] Threatening messages
- [ ] Counselor's letter

## Common Questions We Get

**Q: "What if we're divorced now?"**  
A: You can still file if you apply within 2 years of the divorce.

**Q: "My abuser lost their green card - does that hurt my case?"**  
A: No. Their status when the abuse occurred is what matters.

**Q: "How long until I get work authorization?"**  
A: Typically 6-8 months after filing.
