# USCIS Fee Changes 2025: What to Know Before You File I-765 (EAD) or I-589 (Asylum)

USCIS updated its fee schedule in 2025 based on the H.R.1 reconciliation bill. These changes **directly affect** many people who file for work permits (Form **I-765**) and asylum (Form **I-589**). If you plan to file or renew any of these forms, read this first — filing with the wrong fee can lead to rejection and costly delays.

> **Bottom line:** Certain I-765 applications (asylum-based, parolee, TPS, and related categories) now carry **new mandatory fees**. USCIS also introduced a **new initial $100 asylum filing fee plus an annual $100 Asylum Annual Fee** for pending I-589s. File with the correct payment for any submission postmarked on/after July 22, 2025.

---

## Key takeaways (fast)

- **I-765 (EAD)** — new HR-1 fees apply to asylum, parolee, and certain TPS and humanitarian categories: **initial EAD = $550; renewal/extension = $275** (FY2025 amounts). These fees are mandatory for the affected categories.  
- **I-589 (Asylum)** — USCIS added a **$100 initial asylum filing fee** and a **$100 annual asylum fee (AAF)** that applies while an I-589 remains pending. The agency will start enforcing these fees for filings postmarked on/after **July 22, 2025**; filings without required HR-1 fees may be rejected. 
- **Rejection risk** — USCIS warned it will reject forms postmarked on/after **Aug 21, 2025** that do not include the new required fees; check the USCIS fee schedule and confirmation before mailing. 
- **No HR-1 waiver** — the new HR-1 fees (the additional fees Congress required) generally **cannot be waived**. That means fee-sensitive applicants must budget for these new costs; some older regulatory fees may still be waivable, but not the HR-1 portion. 
- **Use the USCIS Fee Calculator** before filing to confirm exact totals for your packet — multiple forms each require separate payments when the HR-1 fees apply. 

---

## Who is affected?

**Directly affected (examples):**
- Asylum applicants and certain asylum-based EAD filers (EAD category **(c)(8)** and related categories). 
- Parolees and applicants whose EAD basis is parole, TPS registrants, and some humanitarian categories (see USCIS I-765 page for list of codes). 
- New I-589 filers (initial filing) — now require the $100 initial asylum fee plus future calendar-year AAF payments while the case remains pending. 

**Not every I-765 filer** is affected — employment authorization tied to other statuses (certain nonimmigrant work visas where work is authorized by status rather than an EAD, or EAD categories not covered by HR-1) may follow the older fee rules. Always check the Fee Schedule and the I-765 page for your exact eligibility code. 

---

## Exact amounts and dates (what to watch)

- **Initial EAD (affected categories): $550** (FY2025 HR-1 fee).  
- **EAD renewal/extension (affected categories): $275**.  
- **I-589 initial filing fee: $100** (plus **Asylum Annual Fee (AAF)** $100 per calendar year while pending).  
- **Effective filing date to include new fees:** USCIS said forms **postmarked on/after July 22, 2025** must include the new fees. USCIS has warned it will **reject** forms postmarked on/after **Aug 21, 2025** that do not include required HR-1 fees — do not risk mailing without confirmation. 

> **Practical note:** USCIS has updated its G-1055 Fee Schedule and the online Fee Calculator. Use these official tools to calculate the correct filing amount before mailing. 

---

## Why this matters (real consequences)

- **Rejected filings = delays.** A rejected package means you must refile correctly and wait again — sometimes losing priority dates and creating gaps in eligibility (for example, EAD gaps for workers).  
- **No waiver for HR-1 fees.** Advocates have stressed that HR-1 fees cannot be waived, so applicants who previously qualified for fee waivers may now still owe the HR-1 portion. This makes financial planning essential.  
- **Multiple payments for multiple forms.** If you file I-589, I-765, I-131 (travel), or other forms together, each form may require its own HR-1 fee — calculate totals carefully.

---

## Practical filing checklist (before you mail or e-file)

1. **Use the USCIS Fee Calculator (G-1055)** — select each form you plan to file and confirm the total. Screenshot or print the calculator result.  
2. **Confirm your I-765 eligibility code** (Item 27). HR-1 fees apply only to specific EAD categories — double-check the I-765 page.
3. **Include the $100 I-589 initial fee** if filing I-589 on/after July 22, 2025 (and be prepared for annual AAF payments while the case is pending).
4. **If filing multiple forms, pay separately** for each form as instructed. Don’t staple checks to forms unless the instructions allow — follow USCIS payment rules precisely.
5. **Keep proof of mailing and receipts.** Use tracked mail and keep digital copies of everything. If filing online, save confirmation PDFs/screenshots.

## Options if you can’t afford the HR-1 fees

- **Fee waivers do not cover the HR-1 portion.** USCIS has said the additional HR-1 fees are not waivable — though certain legacy regulatory fees may still qualify for Form I-912 waivers. That means many low-income applicants will still owe the HR-1 charge. Consult an attorney or community legal provider about narrow options and timing. 
- **Delay strategy is risky.** Waiting to file in hopes of a policy change is dangerous — if you file after the effective date without payment, USCIS may reject your case. Discuss timing with counsel.

## If USCIS rejects your package for incorrect fees

- **You will receive a rejection notice.** Follow the notice instructions precisely (it will explain whether USCIS will return documents and how to refile). Keep copies of the rejection and all original submission proof.

## Quick FAQs

**Q: Do automatic EAD extensions still apply?**  
A: Some renewal categories allow automatic extensions if you file a timely renewal — but new HR-1 fees change the calculations for certain categories. Always confirm current USCIS guidance before relying on an extension. 

**Q: Can someone pay fees online?**  
A: Yes — many forms (and the Asylum Annual Fee) can be paid online where USCIS provides a payment gateway. Check the specific form page for online payment instructions. 
**Q: Are court (EOIR) fees changing too?**  
A: EOIR (immigration court) fees under HR-1 were also adjusted; if your case involves EOIR filings, confirm EOIR payment rules separately.

## Final advice — before you file

1. **Don’t guess the fee.** Use the G-1055 Fee Schedule / Fee Calculator and confirm the exact amount for your form package.
