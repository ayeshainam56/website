# I Missed My Asylum Hearing. What Happens Now?  

*Missing an asylum hearing can feel like the end of the road — but in many cases, it’s not. Immigration courts take “no-shows” seriously, yet there are legal options to reopen your case if you act quickly and have valid reasons.*  

## Key Takeaways  
✅ **Automatic Order of Removal:** If you miss your hearing, the judge will likely issue an *in absentia* removal order.  
✅ **Valid Excuses Matter:** Medical emergencies, lack of proper notice, or unavoidable circumstances can justify reopening.  
✅ **Time-Sensitive:** You often have **180 days** to file a motion to reopen (or longer if notice was defective).  
✅ **Legal Support Is Critical:** An experienced immigration attorney can argue why you deserve another chance.  

---

## 1. What Happens If You Miss Your Asylum Hearing?  

- The judge may order you **removed in absentia** (in your absence).  
- This order has the same legal effect as if you had attended and lost your case.  
- ICE may begin removal proceedings soon after.  

👉 In some cases, if you miss your hearing because of a government error (e.g., wrong address notice), the order can be overturned.  

---

## 2. Can I Reopen My Case?  

Yes, but you must move quickly:  

### Filing a Motion to Reopen  
- **Deadline:** 180 days from the removal order if your absence was due to “exceptional circumstances” (illness, emergency).  
- **No Deadline:** If you never received proper notice of your hearing.  

### Evidence You’ll Need  
- Medical records (if illness prevented attendance)  
- Proof of emergency (hospital records, accident reports, etc.)  
- Proof of incorrect or missing hearing notice  

---

## 3. What If I Was Detained or Had an Emergency?  

If you were:  
- **Detained by authorities** at the time of hearing → You may qualify for reopening.  
- **Hospitalized or facing medical emergency** → Strong basis for a motion.  
- **Victim of bad legal advice** (lawyer failed to inform you) → Also a valid claim.  

---

## 4. What Are My Next Steps?  

1. **Contact an Attorney Immediately** – Missing a hearing is serious, but not always final.  
2. **Gather Evidence** – Collect documents proving why you missed court.  
3. **File Motion to Reopen** – Your attorney will prepare and submit this to the Immigration Court.  
4. **Prepare for New Hearing** – If granted, you’ll receive a new date to present your asylum case.  

---

## 5. How to Avoid This in the Future  

- Always update your **address with USCIS and the court** (Form EOIR-33).  
- Set **calendar reminders** and request hearing notices by text/email.  
- Work closely with your lawyer to track your case status.  

---

## Final Thoughts  

Missing your asylum hearing is frightening — but it does not always mean the end of your immigration journey. With quick action, valid evidence, and the right legal guidance, many people successfully reopen their cases and continue fighting for protection in the U.S.
